# Evidence that the pjman problem exists and is growing (as of 2026-10-09)

Scope: test the THESIS.md claim — "alignment is hard, execution is easy" — against surveys, telemetry studies, academic research and practitioner reports. Every claim is dated and sourced. **Self-report** (survey) and **measured** (telemetry / RCT / benchmark) data are labelled. Vendor-sourced data is flagged **[vendor]**. The counter-evidence section is deliberately not softened.

Method note: the "DORA takeaways" page published by Faros AI (vendor) blends Faros's own telemetry numbers into a summary of DORA; the two are separated below. Where a number only appeared in a secondary source (press write-up, blog) it is marked **[secondary]**.

---

## Key Question 1 — Where does time now go in AI-assisted software development?

### Takeaway
Across every major 2025–2026 source, AI adoption is near-universal (84–95 %) and developers *report* large time savings, but measured organisational throughput gains are modest (10–20 %) and the saved hours are absorbed by non-coding friction: finding information, context switching, cross-team collaboration and review. Coding was already a small slice of the day (Atlassian: 16 %), and no survey lists it as a friction point — the friction is upstream and downstream of the code.

### Cited Findings

**Adoption and self-reported gains**
- DORA 2025 (survey, n≈5,000, published 2025-09): "90% of survey respondents report using AI at work"; "More than 80% believe it has increased their productivity"; "30% report little or no trust in the code generated by AI". — [Google Cloud announcement of the 2025 DORA report](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)
- DORA 2025 [secondary]: developers spend "a median of two hours per day working with AI tools"; "nearly two-thirds rely on AI for at least half their workflow, and for one in twelve development work is now almost entirely AI-mediated". — [i-programmer write-up](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/105-artificial-intelligence/18354-dora-report-reveals-widespread-reliance-on-ai.html)
- Stack Overflow 2025 (survey, >49,000 respondents, 2025-07): 84 % use or plan to use AI tools (up from 76 % in 2024); 51 % of professional developers use AI daily. — [survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025 (survey, n=24,534, 2025-10) [secondary]: 85 % regularly use AI tools; "nearly nine out of ten save at least an hour every week, and one in five saves eight hours or more"; top reported benefit after productivity: "less time spent searching for information (72%)". — [JetBrains research blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/); [InfoWorld summary](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4077352/85-of-developers-use-ai-regularly-jetbrains-survey.html)
- Atlassian State of Developer Experience 2025 (survey with Wakefield Research, n=3,500 developers and managers, six countries, 2025-07-09): "99% of developers report time savings using AI tools, 68% save more than 10 hours weekly". — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)
- DX AI-assisted engineering Q4 2025 report (self-report, 58,330 developers across 352 companies) [vendor, secondary]: developers save "an average of 3.6 hours per week" (daily users 4.1 h); "nearly doubled since Q4 2024, when the average hovered around two hours"; "gains have plateaued as usage outpaces integration". — [DX report page](https://getdx.com/report/ai-assisted-engineering-q4-impact-report/); [HackerNoon summary](https://hackernoon.com/ai-helps-developers-ship-more-codenot-always-better-code)

**Where the saved time goes (friction)**
- Atlassian 2025: "50% lose 10+ hours per week to non-coding tasks; 90% lose 6+ hours". Top time-wasters: "finding information (services, docs, APIs)", "adapting new technology", "context switching between tools", and "collaboration with other teams (moved up from previous year)"; "coding wasn't listed in 2024 or 2025 as a source of time wasting" although developers spend only 16 % of their time coding; 63 % say leaders don't understand their pain points (44 % in 2024). — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025); [SD Times summary](https://sdtimes.com/ai/report-ai-productivity-gains-cancelled-out-by-friction-points-in-other-areas-that-slow-developers-down/)
- Atlassian State of Teams 2025 (survey, 12,000 knowledge workers + 200 Fortune 1000 executives, 2025-04): workers spend "25% of their workweek" finding information — "nine hours a week" — "2.4 billion hours each year". — [Atlassian State of Teams 2025](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/state-of-teams-2025); [Unleash summary](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/)
- Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 (survey n=31,000 across 31 countries + Microsoft 365 telemetry, 2025-06-17): employees "interrupted every two minutes… 275 disruptions" per day; "ad hoc calls (those without calendar invites) now make up 60% of all meetings"; nearly half say work "feels chaotic and fragmented". — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/microsoft-remote-work-meetings); [ACS Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/workday-now-dominated-by--focus-sapping--meetings.html)

**Measured (not self-reported) gains at the organisation level**
- Stanford (Yegor Denisov-Blanch, >100,000 engineers, >600 companies, commit-level analysis, 2025) [secondary]: "15-20% productivity boost on average"; "30–40%" on low-complexity greenfield tasks, "0–10%" on high-complexity brownfield work; AI "increases rework". — [AI Engineer talk](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/tbDDYKRFjhk-ai-developer-productivity); [Sirris summary](https://www.sirris.be/en/inspiration/how-much-more-productive-does-ai-make-developers)
- Bain Technology Report 2025 (2025-09): teams using AI assistants see "10% to 15% productivity boosts, but often the time saved isn't redirected toward higher-value work"; "even those modest gains don't translate into positive returns". — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/from-pilots-to-payoff-generative-ai-in-software-development-technology-report-2025/)
- METR RCT (measured; 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 tasks, 2025-07): developers "19% slower" with AI (CI +2 % to +39 %) while predicting a 24 % speedup and still believing in a 20 % speedup afterward. Follow-up (2026-02-24): 57 developers, 143 repos, 800+ tasks; returning developers −18 % (CI −38 % to +9 %), new developers −4 % (CI −15 % to +9 %); "30% to 50% of developers told us that they were choosing not to submit some tasks because they did not want to do them without AI"; METR: "we believe it is likely that developers are more sped up from AI tools now — in early 2026 — compared to our estimates from early 2025. However, because of the selection effects in our experiment, our data is only very weak evidence for the size of this increase." — [METR uplift update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- DORA ROI report v2026.01 (2026-05) [secondary]: "The greatest returns on AI investment come not from the tools themselves but from a strategic focus on the underlying organizational system: the quality of the internal platform, the clarity of workflows, and the alignment of teams… Without this foundation, AI creates localized pockets of productivity that are often lost in downstream chaos." (Nathen Harvey). The report models a 39 % first-year ROI for a 500-engineer org with a J-curve dip first; authors call it "a high-uncertainty estimate". — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/05/dora-roi-ai-assisted-dev-report); [dora.dev/ai/roi](https://dora.dev/ai/roi)

### Inferences
- The gap between self-reported savings (10+ h/week, Atlassian) and measured gains (10–20 %, Stanford/Bain) is itself evidence for the thesis: individual execution got faster; the system did not, and the difference is lost to organisational friction, which the surveys name as information-finding, context switching and cross-team collaboration.
- "Localized pockets of productivity… lost in downstream chaos" (DORA) is the closest a neutral research body comes to stating the pjman thesis directly.

### Gaps
- No source decomposes developer time into "collecting context / finding people / extracting decisions" at the granularity THESIS.md uses. Atlassian's "finding information" and "collaboration with other teams" are the nearest proxies.
- No DORA 2026 annual State-of report had been published as of 2026-10-09 (search found only the 2025 trilogy and the 2026.01 ROI report).

## Key Question 2 — Context loss across agent sessions and its cost

### Takeaway
Context loss between sessions is universally acknowledged by vendors and practitioners (every major lab has shipped a memory feature since mid-2025 specifically to address it), and the research literature on "context rot" shows that longer context is not a substitute for curated context. However, there is **no rigorous measurement of the cost of context loss** (hours re-explaining, decisions re-litigated); the evidence is product behaviour, adoption counts and anecdote.

### Cited Findings
- "Context engineering" entered common use on 2025-06-18 when Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke posted it on X ("the art of providing all the context for the task to be plausibly solvable by the LLM"); Andrej Karpathy endorsed it about a week later as "the delicate art and science of filling the context window with just the right information for the next step". — [Simon Willison, 2025-06-27](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/)
- Chroma "Context Rot" (measured benchmark, 2025-07; 18 models incl. GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5): "model reliability decreases significantly with longer inputs, even on simple tasks like retrieval and text replication"; recommends "targeted context engineering". — [Hacker News thread on the Chroma report](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/44564248); [ZenML summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/context-rot-evaluating-llm-performance-degradation-with-increasing-input-tokens)
- Anthropic shipped **auto-memory in Claude Code v2.1.59 (2026-02)**, enabled by default, "to make handoffs between sessions smoother"; persistent memory extended to all Claude users including free tier in early 2026-03. — [MemoryPlugin explainer](https://blog.memoryplugin.com/claude-code-memory/) [secondary]
- OpenAI added **project-scoped persistent memory to Codex on 2026-04-16**; "If you switch projects, you start fresh; if you switch from Codex to ChatGPT, you start fresh again". — [Studio Meyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/codex-memory-mcp-fix) [secondary]
- Third-party demand signal: the open-source `claude-mem` plugin ("Every new Claude Code session starts cold… Ask an agent today what it decided about your database schema three sessions ago and it has no idea") reached 74.8k–83.9k GitHub stars by mid-2026, with v13.1.0 adding a Postgres-backed server for team deployments. — [Augment Code write-up](https://www.augmentcode.com/learn/claude-mem-74k-stars-agent-memory) [vendor, secondary]
- Anthropic's internal study (survey n=132, 53 interviews, 200,000 Claude Code transcripts, data 2025-08, published 2025-12-02): staff use Claude for 59 % of work (28 % a year earlier) but "0-20%" of work is fully delegable; 27 % of Claude-assisted tasks "wouldn't have been done otherwise"; roles shifting toward "manager[s] of AI agents". The study reports **no data on context loss between sessions**. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- Faros 2026 telemetry (measured, 22,000 developers, 4,000+ teams) [vendor]: "Daily PR contexts per developer are up 67.4%", "Work restarts… are up 13.8%", "26% more in-progress tasks show no activity for seven or more days". — [Faros AI Engineering Report 2026 takeaways](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways)

### Inferences
- Every lab treating memory as a first-party feature confirms the problem is real and widely felt; it also means the *facts* layer of memory is being solved natively (see KQ6). What labs ship is per-tool, per-project and per-surface (Codex memory does not cross surfaces) — none persists *decisions, ownership or rationale* across tools, which is the layer THESIS.md claims.
- Faros's rising "work restarts" and "stalled tasks" are consistent with context being lost at hand-offs, but the metrics do not isolate agent-session loss from ordinary interruption.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies hours lost re-explaining context to agents, or the rate at which decisions made in one session are contradicted in a later one. This is the single largest evidentiary hole in the thesis.
- Could not verify Cursor "Memories" launch details from a primary source in this pass (search result conflated it with Codex).

## Key Question 3 — The review/approval bottleneck, "AI slop" and PR volume

### Takeaway
This is the best-evidenced part of the thesis. Three independent telemetry datasets (GitHub, Faros, LinearB) agree that AI roughly doubled PR volume and that review — a human authorization step — absorbed the gain: review time up 91 % (2025) → median +441 % (2026) at Faros; AI PRs merge at 32.7 % vs 84.4 % and wait 4.6× longer for first review at LinearB; GitHub merged 43.2 M PRs/month (+23 % YoY). Quality metrics moved the wrong way (bugs, incidents, churn, duplication). The bottleneck moved to the human gate exactly as THESIS.md states.

### Cited Findings

**Volume (measured)**
- GitHub Octoverse 2025 (Sep 2024–Aug 2025, published 2025-10-28): "43.2 million pull requests merged per month (+23% YoY)"; "518.7 million pull requests merged (+29% YoY)"; "986M commits (+25.1% YoY)"; Copilot coding agent opened "1+ million pull requests… between May 2025 and September 2025", concentrated in established repos; the report labels these "observational signals rather than causal claims". — [GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)
- Google [secondary, reported 2026-04]: ~75 % of new code AI-generated "and then reviewed by engineers", up from ~25 % (2024-10) and ~50 % (late 2025). — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/04/24/ai-is-generating-most-of-googles-code/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-says-75-percent-of-its-new-code-is-now-written-by-ai/)

**Review load and quality (measured, vendor telemetry)**
- Faros AI 2025 "Acceleration Whiplash" [vendor]: tasks completed +21 %, PRs merged +98 %, "PR review time increased 91%, bug rates climbed 9%, and the average PR ballooned 154% in size". — [Faros takeaways](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways); [DEV summary](https://dev.to/kunal_d6a8fea2309e1571ee7/ai-coding-assistant-team-adoption-what-breaks-after-everyone-gets-a-license-2026-179i)
- Faros AI Engineering Report 2026 (two years of telemetry, 22,000 developers, 4,000+ teams) [vendor]: "Epics completed per developer are up 66%"; "Task throughput per developer is up 33.7%"; "Median time to first PR review is up 156.6%"; "Median time in review is up 441.5%"; "Pull requests merged without any review… are up 31.3%"; "Bugs per developer" up 54 %; "Incidents-to-PR ratio is up 242.7%"; "Code churn… has increased 861%"; the report is "not a survey of how developers feel about AI" but "a measurement of what AI is actually producing". — [Faros](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways)
- LinearB 2026 Software Engineering Benchmarks (8.1 M PRs, 4,800 teams, 163,820 contributors, 42 countries) [vendor]: "AI pull requests merge within 30 days 32.7% of the time against 84.4% for unassisted pull requests"; "Agentic pull requests wait 17.6 hours at the 75th percentile against 3.4 hours for unassisted work"; agentic PR size P75 293 lines vs 157; AI PRs carry 10.83 issues vs 6.45 (1.7×); senior reviewers spend 4.3 min per AI suggestion vs 1.2 min; 38 % of reviewers deprioritise AI PRs; "44.7% of organizations don't formally measure AI's impact, yet 76.1% of leaders report productivity gains"; "64.5% indicate internal data is not adequately prepared for AI workflows". — [LinearB library](https://linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development); [LinearB 2026 report PDF](https://assets.linearb.io/image/upload/v1765300694/resources/LinearB_2026_Software_Engineering_Benchmarks_Report.pdf)
- GitClear 2025 (211 M changed lines, Jan 2020–Dec 2024, two-thirds private corporate repos) [vendor]: "8-fold increase in the frequency of code blocks with five or more lines that duplicate adjacent code"; refactored/moved code fell from ~25 % of changed lines (2021) to under 10 % (2024); 2024 was "the first year… where copy/paste exceeded moved code". — [DevClass](https://devclass.com/2025/02/20/ai-is-eroding-code-quality-states-new-in-depth-report/); [GitClear research page](https://public.amplenote.com/ZRbxXhi9FamWYbKEFaD9h7Qh)

**Trust and verification (self-report)**
- Stack Overflow 2025: 46 % distrust AI accuracy (31 % in 2024), 3.1 % "highly trust"; 66 % cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite"; 45.2 % say "debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming"; 75.3 % ask a person "when I don't trust AI's answers"; only 4.4 % say AI handles complex tasks "very well". — [survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Stack Overflow 2026 (30,000+ respondents, published 2026-10-06): 73 % of AI-tool users use them daily, 31 % of daily users 4+ h/day; 66 % use coding agents (Claude Code 66 %, Copilot 59 %); "48% trusted AI when they could easily verify its output, while only 6.6% trusted it for many tasks including important work decisions"; verification behaviours: 76.5 % run answers locally, 63.9 % compare with their codebase, 53 % inspect tests/security; 93 % require source attribution to trust output. — [Stack Overflow blog, 2026 results](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/); [IT Brief summary](https://itbrief.com.au/story/stack-overflow-survey-shows-developers-trust-ai-with-caveats)
- DORA 2025: "AI adoption does continue to have a negative relationship with software delivery stability"; "that acceleration can expose weaknesses downstream. Without robust control systems… an increase in change volume leads to instability." (2024 found a negative throughput relationship; 2025 found positive throughput, still negative stability.) — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)
- Octoverse 2025: "72.6% of developers who use Copilot code review said it improved their effectiveness" (self-report; AI review as a response to the bottleneck). — [GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)

### Inferences
- "PRs merged without any review up 31.3 %" plus "38 % of reviewers deprioritise AI PRs" indicates the human gate is being *bypassed* as well as *overloaded* — accountability without evidence, which is THESIS.md's "rubber stamp" risk in measured form.
- The three vendor datasets have overlapping customer bases and incentives (they sell engineering-metrics tools), but GitHub's volume numbers and DORA's stability finding are independent and consistent.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) measurement of review time per AI PR; CodeRabbit/Uplevel-style quality studies were not located in a 2025–2026 edition in this pass.

## Key Question 4 — Has coordination/alignment cost risen or fallen with agents?

### Takeaway
The evidence is indirect but consistently points to rising coordination cost: Atlassian records "collaboration with other teams" climbing the friction ranking; DORA finds gains lost "in downstream chaos"; Faros measures more context-switching and more stalled work; Anthropic's own engineers report human-to-human knowledge transfer collapsing. No study has measured decision latency in product organisations post-agents; the "decision latency" numbers circulating in 2026 are recycled older consulting data.

### Cited Findings
- Atlassian 2025: "collaboration with other teams has moved up as a friction point"; "Tech debt fell out of the top 5 this year". — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)
- DORA 2025: "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there. Strong teams use AI to become even better and more efficient. Struggling teams will find that AI only highlights and intensifies their existing problems." Seven capabilities that amplify AI's benefit: "Clear and communicated AI stance; Healthy data ecosystems; AI-accessible internal data; Strong version control practices; Working in small batches; User-centric focus; Quality internal platforms"; "90% of organizations have adopted at least one platform". — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report); [DORA AI Capabilities Model](https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model)
- DORA ROI 2026.01: returns depend on "the clarity of workflows, and the alignment of teams". — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/05/dora-roi-ai-assisted-dev-report)
- Anthropic internal study (2025-12): "I ask way more questions... but like 80-90% of them go to Claude"; "More junior people don't come to me with questions as often"; mentorship and knowledge transfer disrupted as Claude becomes "the first stop for questions". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- Faros 2026: "Daily PR contexts per developer are up 67.4%", task contexts +17.7 %, work restarts +13.8 %, stalled tasks +26 %. — [Faros](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways) [vendor]
- Anthropic 2026 Agentic Coding Trends Report (2026-02): trends include "multi-agent coordination" and "cycle times collapse from weeks to hours"; developers "use AI in roughly 60% of their work, but report being able to 'fully delegate' only 0–20% of tasks". — [Anthropic resources](https://resources.anthropic.com/2026-agentic-coding-trends-report) [vendor]
- Multi-agent coordination research [secondary, not independently verified]: a 2026 study of 260 configurations across six benchmarks found hybrid multi-agent systems needed "44.3 reasoning turns versus 7.2 for the single-agent baseline"; "when coding agents were told which one was in charge, no hub ever formed around it, and success didn't reliably improve". — [Agile Brand Guide summary](https://agilebrandguide.com/coordination-has-a-ceiling-and-adding-agents-does-not-raise-it-martech-futurist-august-4-2026/); [paper listing](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16801)
- Decision-latency figures circulating in 2026 ("37% of working time is spent making decisions… 58% of decision time judged ineffective… 61% [fast] at 1–3 reporting layers vs 38% at seven or more") are re-published consulting survey data (McKinsey 2019 origin) on vendor pages, not new 2026 measurements. — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/executive-decision-latency-statistics-2026) [vendor, recycled]

### Inferences
- The strongest reading: agents remove the engineer as the *record* (Anthropic's "questions go to Claude", juniors stop asking seniors) without replacing the record, which is exactly the mechanism THESIS.md names. This is qualitative, from one company, and self-reported.
- DORA's seven capabilities are all organisational-system properties (data, platforms, batch size, stance), not tool properties — supporting "alignment is the bottleneck" without naming it.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 study measures decision latency, approval wait time, or meeting load specifically in software/product organisations after agent adoption. Microsoft's WTI 2025 interruption data is economy-wide.
- No evidence either way that agents *increase* cross-team coordination load causally; Atlassian's ranking shift is suggestive only.

## Key Question 5 — Demand signals for structure (specs, plans, decision records, CLAUDE.md-style files)

### Takeaway
Demand for persisted structure around agents grew sharply from mid-2025: AGENTS.md went from a vendor convention to a Linux Foundation-stewarded standard used by 60k+ open-source projects; GitHub's Spec Kit reached 140.6k stars in roughly a year; academic MSR work found context files are "often the sole mechanism" of agent configuration. But a controlled study shows **auto-generated** context files hurt and even **human-written** ones add only ~4 points — the value lies in curated, human-authored content, which is the slice pjman claims.

### Cited Findings
- AGENTS.md: "used by over 60k open-source projects" (GitHub code-search link on the site); compatible with 20+ agents (Codex, Jules, Cursor, Copilot, Devin, Aider, goose, VS Code, Factory…); "now stewarded by the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation". — [agents.md](https://agents.md) (checked 2026-10-09)
- MSR 2026 paper "Context Engineering for AI Agents in Open-Source Software" (Mohsenimofidi, Galster, Treude, Baltes; submitted 2025-10-24, revised 2026-02-05): mined 10,000 repositories, 466 had adopted at least one AI context file; "no established content structure yet"; context provided in "descriptive, prescriptive, prohibitive, explanatory, conditional" modes; commit analysis shows projects "continuously extend and maintain these files". — [arXiv 2510.21413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21413v4)
- "Harness Engineering for Agentic AI Coding Tools: An Exploratory Study" (Galster et al.; submitted 2026-02-16, revised 2026-06-30; 2,853 GitHub repositories): context files "dominate the configuration landscape and are often the sole mechanism in a repository, with AGENTS.md emerging as an interoperable standard"; Skills and Subagents rarely adopted; Claude Code users show "the broadest range of mechanisms". — [arXiv 2602.14690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14690)
- ETH Zurich / LogicStar "Evaluating AGENTS.md" (Gloaguen, Mündler, Müller, Raychev, Vechev; arXiv 2602.11988; measured benchmark AGENTbench, 138 real tasks, four frontier agents; 2026-02/03): LLM-generated context files **reduced** success by ~3 % and raised inference cost by >20 %; human-written files **raised** success by ~4 % (cost up to +19 %); the article notes "60,000 open-source repositories currently contain context files". — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/agents-context-file-value-review/); [ArXivIQ summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/evaluating-agentsmd-are-repository)
- GitHub Spec Kit (open-sourced 2025-09): 140.6k stars, 12.6k forks as of 2026-10-09; "Define what and why before deciding how to build it. SDD turns your requirements into a specification, a technical plan, and actionable tasks". — [github.com/github/spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit)
- Spec-driven ecosystem: AWS Kiro (2025-07), Spec Kit (2025-09), OpenSpec, BMAD-METHOD, Tessl ("raised $125 million on the premise that specs will become software's source of truth"). — [codemyspec comparison](https://codemyspec.com/blog/spec-driven-development) [secondary/vendor]
- DORA's capability "AI-accessible internal data" and LinearB's "64.5% indicate internal data is not adequately prepared for AI workflows" are demand signals for structured, machine-readable organisational context. — [DORA capabilities](https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model); [LinearB](https://linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development)
- Stack Overflow 2026: 93 % of respondents "require source attribution for trust" — demand for provenance on agent output. — [Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)
- Claude Code adoption (context for the above): Anthropic reports Claude Code went "from being used by 4% of developers to 63%" in under nine months with session lengths "from 4 to 23 minutes" (population not specified in the summary seen) [vendor]; Stack Overflow's April 2026 pulse found agent use "almost doubled to 59%" and Claude Code "from 41% to 55%". — [Anthropic trends report](https://resources.anthropic.com/2026-agentic-coding-trends-report); [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/stack-overflow-survey-shows-developers-trust-ai-with-caveats)

### Inferences
- The ETH result is double-edged for pjman: it validates that human-authored structure (decisions, constraints) is what helps agents, and it warns that any product which *generates* context for agents may make them worse. pjman's "humans author decisions; agents do everything else" tenet is consistent with the finding; a derived-context layer that is fed back to agents is not automatically safe.
- Adoption of AGENTS.md/Spec Kit is a demand signal for *per-repo instructions and per-feature specs*, not yet for cross-tool decision records or ownership graphs; no adoption data was found for ADR-style decision records in agent workflows.

### Gaps
- No count of repositories containing ADRs/decision records in 2025–2026; no survey asks about decision-record practice with agents.
- Spec Kit star count is interest, not sustained use; no retention data found.

## Key Question 6 — Counter-evidence

### Takeaway
There is real evidence against parts of the thesis: labs are shipping memory natively (eroding the "no record" premise for facts), solo founders and tiny teams are shipping large products with no alignment layer at all, the dominant developer response to AI friction is *more verification*, not *more process*, and developers explicitly resist AI in planning and spec-heavy workflows. Also, the whole "AI made review the bottleneck" story rests mostly on vendor telemetry with commercial incentives, and the one RCT (METR) could not even establish a speedup in 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Memory is being solved natively by the labs**
- Claude Code auto-memory shipped by default in v2.1.59 (2026-02); persistent memory to all Claude users (2026-03). — [MemoryPlugin](https://blog.memoryplugin.com/claude-code-memory/) [secondary]
- OpenAI Codex project-scoped persistent memory (2026-04-16). — [Studio Meyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/codex-memory-mcp-fix) [secondary]
- Claude Code's own memory layer (CLAUDE.md + auto memory) is described as "the practical default" for most users; third-party plugins differentiate on cross-tool and team scope only. — [MemoryPlugin](https://blog.memoryplugin.com/claude-code-memory/) [vendor]

**Solo and tiny teams ship without alignment overhead**
- Reports of a one-person telehealth company (Medvi) posting "$401 million in sales in its first full year" with no employees; "Solo-founded startups surged to 36.3% of all new ventures in 2026"; Pieter Levels ">$3 million ARR entirely by himself". — [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/the-first-solo-unicorns-are-here-meet-the-one-person-companies-racing-to-1b-in-2026) **[promotional, unaudited; treat as weak]**
- Anthropic 2026 trends report case studies: "4-8 month projects compressed to 2 weeks". — [Anthropic resources](https://resources.anthropic.com/2026-agentic-coding-trends-report) [vendor]
- Google: a complex code migration "completed six times faster than a year earlier" via agents + developers. — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/04/24/ai-is-generating-most-of-googles-code/) [secondary]

**Developers reject process overhead / planning with AI**
- Stack Overflow 2025: 69.2 % of developers do not plan to use AI for project planning; 75.8 % not for deployment/monitoring. — [survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- AWS added "design-first and bugfix workflows" to Kiro because "most developers don't work the way Kiro forces them to… Kiro started with a vision of spec-driven development, but that's not the situation developers find themselves in". — [CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4135314/aws-adds-design-first-and-bugfix-workflows-to-kiro-2.html)
- Practitioner critiques: "Spec maintenance is real overhead—once you have a spec and an implementation, you now have two things that need to stay in sync"; "writing a detailed specification for a bug fix or a small feature is overkill". — [alexcloudstar, "Spec-Driven Development in 2026: … or Waterfall 2.0?"](https://alexcloudstar.com/blog/spec-driven-development-2026/); [O'Reilly Radar, "The right amount of spec for agentic development"](https://oreilly.com/radar/the-right-amount-of-spec-for-agentic-development)
- Auto-generated context files make agents worse (−3 % success, +20 % cost); even human-written files cost up to +19 % more inference for +4 % success — structure is not free. — [InfoQ on ETH study](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/agents-context-file-value-review/)
- Octoverse 2025: Copilot code review (AI reviewing AI) rated as improving effectiveness by 72.6 % of its users — the market's answer to the review bottleneck is more automation at the gate, not more human structure. — [GitHub Octoverse](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)

**"Execution is easy" is not established**
- METR 2025 RCT: −19 % with AI; 2026 follow-up could not produce a reliable estimate; METR's own banner says the 2025 result is out of date, but the 2026 data gives "only very weak evidence" of improvement. — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- Stanford: 0–10 % gains on complex brownfield work — the kind of work most organisations do. — [AI Engineer talk](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/tbDDYKRFjhk-ai-developer-productivity)
- Stack Overflow 2025: only 4.4 % say AI handles complex tasks "very well"; 39.6 % rate it "bad" or "very poor". Anthropic staff fully delegate only 0–20 % of work. — [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- Bain: adoption among firms that rolled out GenAI tools is "low", and gains "quickly evaporate" without habit formation. — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/from-pilots-to-payoff-generative-ai-in-software-development-technology-report-2025/)

**The review-bottleneck numbers are vendor-sourced and partly inconsistent**
- Faros's 2025 and 2026 reports give different magnitudes for the same metrics (review time +91 % vs median +441.5 %; bugs +9 % vs +54 %), reflecting different windows and cohorts; LinearB finds AI-assisted PRs *clear review faster* at P75 (3.2 h vs 4.2 h) even as agentic PRs wait longer for pickup — the "bottleneck" is pickup/triage more than review effort per se. — [Faros](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways); [LinearB](https://linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development)
- DORA 2025 found *positive* throughput and product-performance relationships with AI (a reversal from 2024) — the organisation-level picture is improving, not only degrading. — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)

### Inferences
- The thesis's weakest claims are (a) "execution is easy" (measured gains are modest and concentrated in greenfield work) and (b) "there is no record" (labs now ship memory). Its strongest claims are the review/authorization bottleneck (three telemetry datasets plus DORA stability) and friction being organisational (Atlassian, DORA, Bain).
- The solo-founder evidence undercuts the *necessity* of an alignment layer for small operators, which is consistent with THESIS.md's own positioning ("A solo developer has no alignment pain") but means the paid tier's market is organisations, not the free tier's users.
- Developer resistance to planning-with-AI and spec overhead means a product that "prescribes what gets recorded" must keep the recording cost near zero or it will be rejected like Kiro's original workflow.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly tests whether structured decision records improve agent outcomes or reduce rework (the ETH study tests instruction files, not decision/ownership records).
- Solo-company revenue claims could not be verified against audited sources.
- No independent replication of Faros/LinearB telemetry findings by a non-vendor body was found.
