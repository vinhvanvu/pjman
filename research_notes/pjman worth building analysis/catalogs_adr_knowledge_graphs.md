# Service catalogs / IDPs, ADR tooling, and enterprise engineering knowledge graphs — adoption and failure modes (as of 2026-10-09)

Scope note for the report writer: this covers the three closest existing analogs to pjman's layers — (a) service catalogs / internal developer portals (ownership + maturity layer), (b) Architecture Decision Record tooling and practice (decision layer), (c) enterprise engineering knowledge graphs for agents ("graph of engineering context" layer) — plus the historical requirements-traceability literature as the precedent for "link declared intent to code". Every claim is dated and sourced; where I could not find a source, it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted. Research was done 2026-10-09 via web search + page fetches; some vendor pages are undated and are flagged as such.

## KQ1. Backstage (Spotify/CNCF): adoption, documented problems, and what has been done about them (incl. 2025–2026 AI/agent additions)

### Takeaway
Backstage is the dominant open-source IDP by a wide margin (3.4k+ known adopters, ~31k GitHub stars, still CNCF *Incubating* as of 2026-10 — not graduated), and its best-documented failure mode is exactly the one pjman names: a hand-authored `catalog-info.yaml` model that is costly to populate, goes stale, and erodes trust. The community's fixes are (1) automated discovery/ingestion providers, (2) scorecards (Soundcheck / Tech Insights) that *measure* catalog entities against standards, and (3) from 2025, an Actions Registry + MCP server so agents can read the catalog — but none of these change the catalog from "hand-declared" to "derived", and none model product features, decisions, or declared-vs-observed drift.

### Cited Findings

**Adoption / status**
- Backstage community stats at end of 2025: "3.4k+ adopters", "1.8k contributors", "15k Discord members", "7k project forks", "68k total contributions", "250+ open source plugins", "31k GitHub stars"; 7.7k commits to backstage/backstage in 2025 — [Backstage Wrapped 2025, backstage.io, 2025-12-30](https://backstage.io/blog/2025/12/30/backstage-wrapped-2025)
- The same post cites a March 2025 DX survey claiming Backstage has "89% market share compared to SaaS competitors" and "67% overall market penetration" for IDPs (vendor-relayed survey; treat as directional) — [Backstage Wrapped 2025](https://backstage.io/blog/2025/12/30/backstage-wrapped-2025)
- CNCF maturity: **Incubating**. Accepted to CNCF 2020-09-08; moved to Incubating 2022-03-15. No graduation as of the fetch on 2026-10-09. The CNCF page's "health score" is listed as "Fair (69/100)" and its star/fork counters are clearly stale/mismatched vs. GitHub (shows 3,749 stars) — [CNCF project page](https://www.cncf.io/projects/backstage/). (Backstage Wrapped 2025's footer also references "CNCF Incubation".)
- In March 2026 CNCF released a documentary on Backstage's evolution "from development to becoming a global open source standard for platform engineering" — search summary of [CNCF announcements page](https://www.cncf.io/news/announcements/page/2/) (I did not fetch the documentary page itself; treat the exact wording as unverified).
- Spotify's own scale: Soundcheck "fundamentally changed Spotify's tech health ecosystem, moving from using spreadsheets to monitor 30,000+ components to incentivizing code alignment to engineering best practices in real time" — [Spotify roadmap post, backstage.spotify.com, May 2023](https://backstage.spotify.com/blog/spotify-roadmap-for-backstage-may-2023)
- Gartner: "80% of large engineering organizations will establish platform teams by 2026, up from 45% in 2022, and by 2028, 85% of those platform teams will provide Internal Developer Portals" (Gartner Market Guide for Internal Developer Portals, published 2025-03-31, as relayed by Port) — [Port summary of the Gartner Market Guide](https://www.port.io/blog/some-takeaways-gartners-market-guide-for-internal-developer-portals); Gartner abstract at [gartner.com/doc/6306515](https://www.gartner.com/doc/6306515)
- Internal developer platform adoption "jumped from 23% in Q3 2024 to 27% in 2025" (secondary source; original survey not verified) — [byteiota](https://byteiota.com/platform-engineering-hits-55-the-adoption-tipping-point/)
- Roadie ran a 2025 "State of Backstage" survey with "105 active Backstage practitioners across industries, company sizes, and maturity levels"; its announced scope included "Catalog health" — [Roadie, 2025 State of Backstage report announcement](https://roadie.io/blog/the-2025-state-of-backstage-report/); survey launch post dated 2025-03-14 at [tfir.io](https://tfir.io/survey-understanding-backstage-and-internal-developer-portals-in-2025/). The headline statistics are in a PDF I could not fetch (see Gaps).

**Documented failure modes — staleness and authoring burden**
- Port (competitor, so biased, but the critique is the standard one): "The main way to populate the Backstage software catalog is by manually creating static YAML files, which requires a lot of effort from the entire organization and poses an adoption challenge even before the portal is launched"; "Manual work also creates maintainability issues, and, most importantly, can pose a significant adoption challenge"; the catalog "doesn't include real-time data... If it isn't [up to date], trust in the catalog erodes"; YAML updates "must be maintained separately from code changes, causing outdated information"; the entity model (Component, Resource, API, User, Group, System, Domain) "is part of the system's core, which means it cannot be changed without significant coding"; only "~20 Spotify-vetted plugins exist among 120 non-vetted options" (undated page) — [Port: technical disadvantages of Backstage](https://port.io/blog/what-are-the-technical-disadvantages-of-backstage)
- Roadie (Backstage vendor, so this is an admission from the pro side): "We have noticed that getting the catalog info file right is difficult. Small syntax errors can mean that the catalog-info.yaml file doesn't appear in Backstage at all. This leads to frustrating hours lost to debugging." — [Roadie changelog](https://roadie.io/changelog/3); Roadie later shipped "Automatic validation of catalog-info files" — [Roadie changelog](https://roadie.io/changelog/automatic-validation-catalog-info-files) and a feature to "Update the Backstage catalog instantly without touching any YAML" — [Roadie](https://roadie.io/tags/scale)
- Red Hat's Developer Hub (a Backstage distribution) tracks catalog-ingestion issues as product bugs, e.g. [RHIDP-6938](https://issues.redhat.com/browse/RHIDP-6938), [RHIDP-3942](https://issues.redhat.com/browse/RHIDP-3942) (titles only seen in search; not fetched).
- Harness, in positioning IDP 2.0 (2025), explicitly lists "Easier automation without YAML file: Entities can now be created and modified inline without having to deal with the YAML file git operations and its complexities" and "Bi-directional Git sync of entity YAML files" — i.e., a Backstage-derived vendor treating the YAML-in-git model as the pain point — [Harness IDP 2.0 overview](https://developer.harness.io/docs/internal-developer-portal/idp-2o-overview/2-0-overview-and-upgrade-path)
- Cortex, in its Compass-migration pitch (2026), frames its differentiator as catalog freshness: "Cortex's Context Graph scans your repositories in the background as soon as the first integration is connected, mapping services, dependencies, and ownership automatically"; "For unowned services, AI-powered ownership prediction fills the gaps from repository activity"; "it stays current as your environment changes, so nothing drifts between audits" — [Cortex on Compass EOL](https://www.cortex.io/post/atlassian-compass-end-of-life-what-to-do-next). The fact that every commercial IDP markets against staleness is itself evidence the failure mode is real and widely felt.
- Port CEO Zohar Einy (TechCrunch, 2025-12-11) characterises Backstage as requiring significant build-it-yourself effort — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/11/port-raises-100m-at-800m-valuation-to-take-on-spotifys-backstage)

**What Spotify / the community did about it**
- Scorecards: Soundcheck (Spotify's commercial plugin) uses a "Fact Framework" to collect "facts (un-opinionated information) about an entity in the software catalog", e.g. a GitHub fact collector; integrates with GitHub/GitLab/Bitbucket/Gerrit — [Spotify roadmap post, May 2023](https://backstage.spotify.com/blog/spotify-roadmap-for-backstage-may-2023). Open-source equivalent is Tech Insights; Roadie's doc shows a scorecard built from a "GitHub Repository Settings Data Source indicating whether a service has CodeOwners enabled" — [Roadie: creating a scorecard](https://roadie.io/docs/getting-started/creating-a-scorecard/)
- 2025 platform additions: "Actions Registry launch enabling AI developer integration" and "MCP (Model Context Protocol) server support debut"; New Backend System v1.0; New Frontend System "adoption-ready"; Backstage UI design system alpha — [Backstage Wrapped 2025](https://backstage.io/blog/2025/12/30/backstage-wrapped-2025)
- Spotify Portal (commercial Backstage) "exposes its plugin capabilities as MCP tools that any compatible AI agent can discover and use. Whether you're using AiKA, Claude Code, Cursor, or VS Code Copilot, the same tools are available"; implemented via `@backstage/plugin-mcp-actions-backend`; each plugin registers with the Actions Registry; endpoint `https://<your-portal>/api/mcp-actions/v1`; example tool sources are Catalog, Soundcheck, Scaffolder; auth via static tokens or CIMD (undated docs page) — [Spotify Portal MCP docs](https://backstage.spotify.com/docs/portal/core-features-and-plugins/mcp); API reference [@backstage/plugin-mcp-actions-backend](https://backstage.io/api/next/modules/_backstage_plugin-mcp-actions-backend.html)
- Community MCP servers for Backstage provide "tools for querying the service catalog, fetching TechDocs, looking up ownership, exploring dependency graphs, and scaffolding new services" — [mcp.so Backstage MCP example](https://mcp.so/servers/mcp-backstage-example)

### Inferences
- Backstage's catalog is *declared* state (owner, lifecycle, system, dependsOn) with no mechanism to compare it against *observed* state; the staleness problem is the direct consequence of a hand-maintained declaration with no drift surfacing. Scorecards partially close this — they compute facts from repos and compare against a standard — but they compare *observed code facts* against *policy*, not observed state against the catalog's own declared claims.
- The 2025 Actions Registry + MCP work makes Backstage a *tool provider* to agents (read catalog, scaffold), not a context graph that agents write to or that records decisions. Nothing found suggests Backstage models product features, maturity-as-claim-plus-evidence, or decisions as first-class entities.
- The market has not "rejected" the catalog shape — it has rejected *hand-authored YAML* as the population mechanism; every commercial player's pitch since ~2023 is auto-discovery and ownership inference. That is consistent with pjman's "derived, not hand-maintained" claim being the right direction, but it also means the derived-ownership idea is already table stakes at the service layer (see KQ2).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the Roadie 2025 State of Backstage PDF, so I have no survey percentages for catalog completeness, time-to-value, or stated challenges.
- I found no first-party Spotify statement quantifying internal catalog staleness or ownership-accuracy, nor an authoritative independent count of failed Backstage rollouts. The "rollout failure" narrative is well attested in vendor material and anecdote but I found no neutral survey quantifying it.
- No Hacker News thread was surfaced by search; candid practitioner quotes here are from vendors (Roadie, Port, Cortex) rather than neutral users.
- I did not verify whether Backstage has applied for CNCF graduation in 2026.

**Decision-record features across the category**
- The only decision-related artefact found in the IDP category is the Backstage ADR *plugin*, which "adds an entity view that lists ADR files from your repo and shows title, status, and date", with search across the catalog — [Roadie plugin page](https://roadie.io/backstage/plugins/architecture-decision-records/). ### Inferences
- "Derived ownership" at the *service/repo* level is now a commodity: Cortex (repo-activity prediction), OpsLevel (recommended owners from git), Compass (suggested owner teams from repo + Confluence + Jira). pjman's differentiation cannot be "we derive owners from CODEOWNERS"; it must be that ownership attaches to *product features* (a tree that does not exist in any repo), that mappings to code are derived while feature nodes are authored, and that drift between the authored claim and the observed mapping is surfaced.
- Every IDP's "maturity" is a scorecard: boolean/graded checks against policy (has owner, has on-call, has CODEOWNERS, passes SLO). None models maturity as a *claim* with *graded evidence* (telemetry > simulation > analysis) nor flags "claimed scaled on analysis-grade evidence" as drift. That specific construct appears unoccupied.
- 2025–2026 repositioning (Port "Context Lake", DX "AI-native intelligence layer", OpsLevel "Tidra AI", Spotify Portal MCP) shows the category racing to be the agent's context source — the same slot pjman wants — but all of them are centred on *services/infra*, not *product intent*.
- Atlassian's exit from a standalone catalog (Compass → DX) and the fact that scorecards do not even migrate is a cautionary datum: catalogs are sticky as a *shape* but weak as a *product* when they are hand-maintained adjuncts to a bigger suite.

## KQ3. ADR tooling and practice: adoption evidence, tools, agent-queryability, and documented reasons ADRs fail

### Takeaway
ADRs are the accepted "right way" to record decisions (Thoughtworks Radar *Adopt* since Nov 2017) yet are used by roughly half of practitioners and are overwhelmingly abandoned or ceremonial in practice: ~50% of GitHub repos with ADRs have 1–5 records, 63% of 5,800 ADRs were opened already "accepted", and practitioners report "almost none" of their teams still maintain them two years in. The documented failure reasons — not maintained, not found at decision time, not linked to code, no operational model for when/who — are structural, not cultural. By 2025–2026 a small ecosystem of MCP servers makes markdown ADRs agent-queryable, but they are read-only indexes of static files, not a decision model bound to owners, features, or code.

### Cited Findings

**Adoption evidence**
- Thoughtworks Technology Radar, "Lightweight Architecture Decision Records": Trial (Nov 2016, Mar 2017) → **Adopt** (Nov 2017, May 2018); recommends storing ADRs in version control "rather than wikis... keeps documentation synchronized with code changes" — [Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/lightweight-architecture-decision-records)
- IcePanel "State of Software Architecture 2024" survey: 96 practitioners (mostly architects/engineers at medium–large companies); on using ADRs "it was a close, even split — 47% said yes, and 53% did not" — [IcePanel, 2024-11-26](https://icepanel.io/blog/2024-11-26-state-of-software-architecture-2024)
- Buchgeher et al., "Using Architecture Decision Records in Open Source Projects — An MSR Study on GitHub", IEEE Access vol. 11 (2023): "adoption of ADRs is still low, although the number of repositories using ADRs is increasing every year"; "About 50% of all repositories with ADRs contain just one to five ADRs"; where sustained, "recording decisions is a team activity conducted by two or more users over a longer period of time"; Nygard template dominant — [JKU publication page](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/using-architecture-decision-records-in-open-source-projects-an-ms/)
- ICSA 2026 paper "Architecture Decision Records: Adoption, Impact, and Developer Engagement in Open-Source Software" (de Santana Junior et al.): 921 GitHub repositories, 5,800 ADRs; "approximately 63% of ADRs (3,674 records) were opened directly with a status of 'accepted' or equivalent", bypassing deliberation; ADR attributes showed "predominantly small correlations with project quality metrics such as code smells and issue resolution time" — [ICSA 2026 paper page](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou)
- A 2026 arXiv study compares ADR templates on comprehension/usability/ease of adoption ("One Size Fits All?") — [arXiv 2604.27333](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27333) (not fetched; title only)
- Red Hat and AWS publish ADR guidance (Red Hat blog [here](https://redhat.com/en/blog/architecture-decision-records)); Sourcegraph keeps its ADRs public in-repo, e.g. "1. Record architecture decisions" — [Sourcegraph ADR](https://3.41.sourcegraph.com/dev/adr/1650968652-record-architecture-decisions)

**Documented failure reasons (practitioner accounts)**
- Hidekazu Konishi (practitioner blog, first published 2026-05-08, updated 2026-09-09): "Almost every team I have worked with has tried to adopt Architecture Decision Records at some point. Almost none of them are still maintaining them two years later." Three failure modes: (1) "Format chosen without operational model" — no rule for when ADRs are required, who reviews, where they live; (2) discoverability — "If the records live in a Confluence space that nobody opens, in a SharePoint folder, or in a Notion workspace separate from the codebase, the records will not be read at decision time"; (3) wrong scope — trivial choices recorded, load-bearing ones skipped — [hidekazu-konishi.com](https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/architecture_decision_records_templates_and_operations.html). (Observational, not empirical.)
- Search-summary of practitioner commentary (javacodegeeks, 2026): ADRs are "praised universally and practised sporadically"; "ADRs are static documents pretending to be living artefacts — and that category error is the real problem"; the problem arises "when organisations adopt ADRs as their primary mechanism for architectural knowledge continuity — a role they were never designed to fill on their own" — [javacodegeeks](https://www.javacodegeeks.com/?p=143468) (not fetched; quotes from search snippet)
- InfoQ on a framework for architectural decisions (undated in results) — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/articles/framework-architectural-decisions/)

**Tools**
- `adr-tools` (Nat Pryce): CLI for Nygard-style ADRs; generates/maintains indices — [Konishi summary](https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/architecture_decision_records_templates_and_operations.html)
- Log4brains: "docs-as-code knowledge base" — markdown ADRs in git published as a searchable static site; CLI, hot-reload preview, GitHub/GitLab Pages/S3 publishing — [Log4brains docs index](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/thomvaill/log4brains)
- Backstage ADR plugin: lists ADR files from the repo with title/status/date next to the owning service; search across the catalog — [Roadie plugin page](https://roadie.io/backstage/plugins/architecture-decision-records/)
- Agent-queryable ADRs (2025–2026): `@adrkit/mcp` — "a local, read-only Model Context Protocol server that lets an agent retrieve prior decisions — including the rejected and superseded ones — before proposing something already tried" — [glama.ai adrkit](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/mbeacom/adrkit); `AdrMcp` (.NET) — "turns a folder of Markdown ADRs into live tools your AI agent can use: search, author, validate, link, and trace decisions" — [nuget AdrMcp](https://nuget.org/packages/adrmcp); `mcp-adr-analysis-server` — [glama.ai](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@tosin2013/mcp-adr-analysis-server/blob/a7f5b1381ab1b6e7309dd1248825330aae9add86/docs/reference/usage-examples.md); an "adr" skill for Claude Code plugins — [skillselion](https://skillselion.com/skills/musingfox/cc-plugins/adr); a Rust `adrs-core` crate — [docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/adrs-core/latest)

### Inferences
- The ADR failure literature maps one-to-one onto pjman's design claims: *not found at decision time* → pjman puts decisions in the graph agents query; *not linked to code* → pjman's `decided`/`affects` edges and derived mappings; *no operational model for when/who* → pjman's derived approvers and bounded asks; *opened already "accepted"* → pjman separates authorizing intent from accepting the result. This is supportive evidence that the pain is real and structurally unsolved.
- The 2025–2026 ADR-MCP servers show demand for "agents read decisions", but they index a folder of markdown with no owner, no feature binding, no evidence, and no drift — they are the thinnest possible version of pjman's decision layer and prove the *interface* (MCP) rather than the *model*.
- The ICSA 2026 "63% opened as accepted" finding is the strongest quantitative signal that the record-of-decision is usually written *after* the fact as documentation, not *as* the decision — which is also the key risk for pjman: a decision record only has value if it is the artefact through which the decision is actually made.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal survey quantifying ADR *abandonment* in industry (as opposed to open source) was found; the "two years" claim is anecdotal.
- No Thoughtworks Radar entry on ADRs more recent than 2018 was found; it has not been re-blipped (which Radar treats as "settled", not "rejected").
- I did not find any decision-log *product* (commercial) with owner/approval routing beyond generic doc tools; absence not exhaustively verified.

## KQ4. Enterprise engineering knowledge graphs for agents: Blitzy, Unblocked, Sourcegraph, Glean (and Atlan) — what the graph holds: declared intent or only observed state?

### Takeaway
The well-funded "engineering context graph" players (Blitzy $200M at $1.4B, May 2026; Unblocked $20M Series A, May 2025; Glean >$200M ARR, Dec 2025; Sourcegraph Amp/Deep Search) all build their graph from **observed** artefacts — code, PRs, Slack, Jira, docs — and surface it to agents via MCP. None found holds *declared* intent as a typed, authored layer (owners of features, maturity claims, decisions with approvers); where "decisions" appear, they are mined from chat/PR history, not authored and routed.

### Cited Findings
- **Blitzy**: $200M at $1.4B valuation, 2026-05-05, led by Northzone, with PSG, Battery, Jump, Morgan Creek Digital, Defiant, plus insurers Liberty Mutual Strategic Ventures, Erie Strategic Ventures, BAL Ventures; "reverse-engineers existing environments, builds a dynamic knowledge graph of the enterprise codebase, and coordinates thousands of agents in parallel for uninterrupted runs lasting days to weeks"; codebases "1 million to over 100 million lines"; ">100,000" model calls per run; 66.5% on SWE-Bench Pro; "dozens of Global 2000 enterprises in 10 industries"; no named customers; nothing on requirements/decisions/ownership as inputs — [BusinessWire 2026-05-05](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260505342338/en/Blitzy-Raises-200-Million-at-1.4-Billion-Valuation) (403 on fetch; details via [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/05/blitzy-raises-200m-1-4b-valuation-deploy-thousands-coding-agents-parallel/) and [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/blitzy-200-million-at-1-4-billion-valuation-raised-to-advance-autonomous-software-development/)). The graph is explicitly "of the enterprise codebase" — observed state only.
- **Unblocked**: $20M USD (~$27.5M CAD) Series A led by B Capital with Radical Ventures and Artisanal Ventures, announced ~2025-05-08; <20 employees, planning 40–50 hires; positioned as "the context engine for modern engineering teams"; CEO Dennis Pilarinos: AI "can write syntax" but "often omits the code reasoning and trade-offs" — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/unblocked-raises-27-5-million-series-a-to-make-sure-developers-actually-understand-their-code/); [BuiltIn](https://builtin.com/articles/unblocked-secures-series-a-funding-20250508). Product reads "across code, PRs, Slack, Jira, design docs, and incident threads, then surfaces decision-grade context to IDEs, agents, and developer workflows through native integrations, an MCP server, a CLI, and APIs"; claims "48%" token reduction and "83% faster" tasks on internal benchmarks (vendor) — [Unblocked blog](https://getunblocked.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-engineering-teams/). Note the phrase "decision-grade context" — decisions are *mined* from history, not authored.
- **Glean**: "surpassed $200M in ARR as of December 2025, doubling revenue in nine months", "100+ sources spanning every department" — as relayed by Unblocked's comparison page [getunblocked.com](https://getunblocked.com/blog/unblocked-vs-glean/) (vendor-relayed; Glean primary announcement not fetched). Unblocked's framing: "Pick Glean when the use case is company-wide knowledge search, not engineering-specific reasoning."
- **Sourcegraph**: "code-intelligence platform for large, multi-repo estates, pairing code search with AI Deep Search and agentic batch changes"; standalone agent Amp "runs pay-as-you-go with no subscription and a 5 USD minimum, billed at model cost" (as described by Unblocked, 2026) — [getunblocked.com](https://getunblocked.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-engineering-teams/). Sourcegraph also dogfoods in-repo ADRs — [Sourcegraph ADR](https://3.41.sourcegraph.com/dev/adr/1650968652-record-architecture-decisions)
- **Augment Code** appears in the same comparison set as an IDE-centric context engine ("Pick Augment when the IDE is the center of gravity") — [getunblocked.com](https://getunblocked.com/blog/unblocked-vs-glean-vs-augment/)
- **IDPs as agent context** (overlap with KQ2): Port's "Context Lake" (data sources, context memory, guardrails for agents) — [TechCrunch 2025-12-11](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/11/port-raises-100m-at-800m-valuation-to-take-on-spotifys-backstage); Spotify Portal MCP — [docs](https://backstage.spotify.com/docs/portal/core-features-and-plugins/mcp); Cortex "Context Graph" — [Cortex](https://www.cortex.io/post/atlassian-compass-end-of-life-what-to-do-next); Atlassian DX "AI-native intelligence layer" — [Cortex summary](https://www.cortex.io/post/atlassian-compass-end-of-life-what-to-do-next)
- **Atlan**: not researched in depth; it is a *data* catalog/governance product (data assets, lineage, owners) rather than an engineering-product graph. No evidence found that it models software features or decisions. (Treat as out of scope; see Gaps.)

### Inferences
- The context-graph players and the IDPs are converging on the same slot ("the thing agents read before acting") from opposite ends: context engines index everything observed; IDPs expose a hand-declared service model. Neither holds an *authored product-intent layer* (feature tree, owners of features, maturity claims, decisions with approvers) nor a drift object between that layer and the observed one. This is the gap pjman's thesis names ("Nobody owns [declared state] today"), and the research supports that it is genuinely unoccupied as of 2026-10 — with the caveat that every one of these players has the distribution and capital to add an authored layer if it proves valuable.
- Blitzy's $1.4B valuation on a graph "of the codebase" signals investor belief that a *derived* engineering graph is a venture-scale asset; pjman's bet is that the derived graph is a commodity and the authored half is the moat. The research cannot settle that; it can only say nobody has shipped the authored half.
- Unblocked's "decision-grade context" is the nearest competitor to pjman's decision layer in spirit, but it is reconstructive (mines why from Slack/PRs) rather than prescriptive (humans author decisions that route to owners). Reconstruction improves as models improve; pjman's durability test implies the authored record is what stays valuable.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Glean's or Sourcegraph's primary pages for 2026 product specifics (e.g., whether Glean "for engineering" or Sourcegraph Deep Search has any notion of ownership or decision entities); the characterisation relies on a competitor's comparison page.
- No Blitzy customer names, and no detail on whether Blitzy ingests specs/requirements as inputs.
- Atlan not researched; other possible players (Greptile, Qodo, Augment's context engine, Moderne) not covered.

## KQ5. Has anyone built "declared vs observed drift" for product features? Traceability literature and why it historically failed

### Takeaway
I found no shipped product that models a product-feature tree with owners and maturity claims and surfaces drift between that declaration and the observed code/deploy state; the closest ideas are (a) Terraform/GitOps drift (infrastructure, not product intent), (b) 2025–2026 "spec-driven development" tooling, which treats the spec as source of truth but is per-feature, per-agent-session, and has no ownership/approval/evidence model, and (c) decades of requirements-traceability work, whose industrial record is that manually maintained links between requirements and code "quickly become outdated" and are abandoned because of cost, tool fragmentation, team scope, and no incentive to maintain cross-artefact links.

### Cited Findings
- **Industrial traceability failure**: Fucci, Alégroth, Axelsson, "When Traceability Goes Awry: an Industrial Experience Report", Journal of Systems and Software (arXiv 2206.04462, 2022). In a large telecom company, the "fundamental traceability links between requirements and test cases were found to be missing"; causes: multiple incompatible systems where "None offered out-of-the-box integration towards any of the other systems"; teams "reluctant to maintain artifacts outside their scope"; manual maintenance "deemed resource-intensive relative to perceived value"; at millions of lines and thousands of developers "maintaining end-to-end traceability became impractical"; agile shift "reduced documentation priorities"; "horizontal traceability" valued over "vertical traceability"; separate organisations with "no incentive for cross-artifact linking". Key finding: the company shipped complex software successfully *without* requirement-to-code-to-test links, "contradicting academic assumptions that such links are essential" — [ar5iv 2206.04462](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.04462)
- **Survey of why practitioners do not trace**: Ruiz et al., "Why don't we trace?" (Requirements Engineering journal, 2023, ZHAW) — fetched as PDF but the text could not be parsed by the fetch tool; search-snippet summary: barriers are cost, complexity, tool inadequacy, organisational culture, weak collaboration between end-users and tool providers; "Manual traceability requires considerable human effort, which often leads to trace links that quickly become outdated"; "Many organizations still do not understand the principles of traceability" — [ZHAW PDF](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/30590/1/2023_Ruiz-etal_Why-dont-we-trace_RequirementsEngineering.pdf) (quotes are from the search engine's summary of this and related sources; verify before quoting verbatim)
- **Spec-driven development (2025–2026)**: positioning text in the SDD ecosystem names the exact failure: "the gap between what a team intended to build and what the code actually does... requirements documents become historical fiction"; "By the time code ships, the ticket, the doc, and the code have all drifted, and six months later nobody can say why a decision was made"; SDD's answer is "the specification is the source of truth; the code is a derived artifact" — [codemyspec.com SDD guide, 2026](https://codemyspec.com/blog/spec-driven-development); [Medium: spec-driven development](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/spec-driven-development-when-intent-becomes-the-source-code-3af39f86b9d3); [dev.to](https://dev.to/rosgluk/spec-driven-development-vs-vibe-coding-waterfall-3e7i); [developertoolkit.ai](https://developertoolkit.ai/en/workflows/plan/spec-driven-development/). None of these describe owners, approvers, evidence grades, or a persistent product tree — they are per-feature spec files driving an agent.
- **IDP "drift" language**: Cortex uses "so nothing drifts between audits" for *catalog freshness vs. repos* (service metadata, not product intent) — [Cortex](https://www.cortex.io/post/atlassian-compass-end-of-life-what-to-do-next). Backstage's scorecards compare repo facts to *policy*, not to the entity's own declaration (KQ1).
- **Ownership-file drift as a scorecard**: Roadie Tech Insights and Compass both treat "has a CODEOWNERS file" as a check — i.e., the industry's current "declared ownership vs observed" is a boolean file-presence test — [Roadie](https://roadie.io/docs/getting-started/creating-a-scorecard/); [Atlassian community](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Compass-questions/Scorecard-criteria-based-on-files-in-git-repos/qaa-p/3036899)
- (From KQ3) ADR tooling's "trace decisions" capability in AdrMcp is link-tracing between ADR markdown files, not to code — [nuget AdrMcp](https://nuget.org/packages/adrmcp)

### Inferences
- The traceability literature is the strongest historical warning for pjman: *hand-maintained* links between intent and code have failed repeatedly for the same reasons the catalogs go stale (manual effort, no integration, no one owns the cross-artefact link, no incentive). pjman's thesis anticipates exactly this ("Mappings are derived; nodes never are"; "derivation is code where it can be and the model only where it must be"). The research supports that the *only* historically viable shape is one where the link is machine-derived and the human authors only the small set of things machines cannot (the feature's existence, its owner, the decision). Whether the derived mapping is accurate enough to be trusted is unproven and is the core technical risk.
- The 2022 Fucci et al. finding that the telecom shipped fine without traceability is a demand-side caution: organisations tolerated missing links because humans carried intent in their heads. pjman's bet is that agents remove that carrier — consistent with its pitch ("Engineers were not only the labour; they were the record") — but no study yet measures whether agent-built organisations need the links more.
- The SDD wave (2025–2026) is the nearest "market pull" signal for declared intent as a first-class artefact and is largely tool-free and solo-developer-scale; it has not addressed multi-owner alignment, approvals, or maturity. That is roughly pjman Phase 1 territory, and it suggests Phase 1's wedge (a local, file-based intent record for agents) will face many lightweight competitors, while Phases 2–3 (owners, approvers, evidence, drift) do not yet have a named competitor.
- Absence finding, stated explicitly: as of 2026-10-09 I found **no** vendor or open-source project — IDP, ADR tool, context engine, or SDD tool — that (1) models product features as an authored tree with per-feature owners, (2) records maturity as a claim with graded evidence, (3) routes decisions to derived approvers, or (4) surfaces drift between that declared layer and observed code/deploy state as a first-class object. The closest fragments each exist separately: derived service ownership (IDPs), scorecards against policy (IDPs), markdown ADRs via MCP (ADR tools), observed-only context graphs (Blitzy/Unblocked), per-feature specs (SDD).

### Gaps
- The academic traceability literature is far larger than two papers; I did not survey automated trace-link recovery (IR/ML-based) results, which would bear directly on whether pjman's "derived mapping" is feasible. Recommend a dedicated pass if the report writer needs a feasibility verdict.
- I did not search patents or product-management tools (Productboard, Aha, Jira Product Discovery) for "feature ↔ code drift" features; the absence claim above is limited to the IDP/ADR/context-graph categories researched.
- The Ruiz et al. 2023 paper's method (sample size, companies) could not be extracted due to PDF parsing failure.
- No Hacker News or neutral practitioner thread was found that discusses feature-level declared-vs-observed drift; the SDD sources are advocacy content.
